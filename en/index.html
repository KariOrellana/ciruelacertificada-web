Sustainability standard for the dehydrated prune industry

A management tool for companies in the sector to identify good practices and actions that can be implemented in the short and medium term, making their processes more sustainable.

What is it about?

The standards group their actions into topics, organised in 5 dimensions.

Environment

Water, Soil, Biodiversity, Inputs, Waste, Energy, Greenhouse gases

Quality

Quality management, Food safety management

Management

Economic viability

Social

Local communities, Working conditions and social protection

Ethics

Due diligence of the law

Classification levels

Each action is classified into 4 levels and earns points according to its priority. A company’s score depends on the actions it complies with at the time of the audit.

5 points

Fundamental

Actions that are essential to qualify a company as sustainable, regardless of their complexity. Their mandatory nature is higher than the rest.

2 points

Basic

Actions with lower impact on sustainability and lower implementation complexity.

3 points

Intermediate

Actions with intermediate impact and implementation complexity.

4 points

Advanced

Actions with the greatest impact on sustainability and the greatest implementation complexity.

Certification periods

To be certified you must meet two conditions: (1) 80% of the score of the fundamental actions and (2) a percentage of the score of the general actions (basic, intermediate and advanced). The more general actions you comply with, the longer your certification.

Primary production

  • 1 year: 80% of fundamental actions + 20% of general actions
  • 2 years: 80% of fundamental actions + 40% of general actions
  • 3 years: 80% of fundamental actions + 60% of general actions

Agro-industrial adaptation

  • 1 year: 80% of fundamental actions + 40% of general actions
  • 2 years: 80% of fundamental actions + 60% of general actions
  • 3 years: 80% of fundamental actions + 80% of general actions

Steps to certification

The path to certify your orchard or plant, from self-assessment to renewal.

  1. 1

    Join the APL

    Joining the Clean Production Agreement (APL) is free of charge. Each facility (orchard or plant) joins and is audited separately, and has one year to complete the cycle.

  2. 2

    Initial self-assessment

    Complete your self-assessment on the online platform and see your level of compliance. Actions that do not apply to your facility are marked “Not compliant”.

  3. 3

    Implementation plan

    Draw up a plan to close the gaps found, with ongoing technical support. If you need to, you can request an intermediate audit before the final one.

  4. 4

    Final audit

    Choose an auditor accredited by the ASCC, who reviews your means of verification, visits your facility and writes the report.

  5. 5

    Public validation

    The public bodies that validate the APL review the reports. Clarifications and corrections are handled with the ASCC within 90 days.

  6. 6

    ASCC certification

    You obtain your certificate for 1, 2 or 3 years, depending on the level reached. When it expires, it is renewed by repeating the self-assessment and the audit.
